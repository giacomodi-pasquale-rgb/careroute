# NearSignal Access Command Center demonstration

## What is operational today

- Patient-side adult and pediatric care navigation across the verified pilot network.
- Access-confidence evidence, source links, travel calculations when routing is available, affordability pathways, multilingual interface, patient-controlled arrival brief, and private device-local outcome follow-up.
- A synthetic institutional dashboard that demonstrates how aggregated outcomes could reveal fragmentation patterns.
- A device-local Verified Arrival simulation that demonstrates consent, partner response, and automatic rerouting.

## What is deliberately simulated

The Command Center metrics are synthetic. The Verified Arrival response is generated locally. No patient information is transmitted, no named facility confirms availability, no appointment is created, and no care is reserved.

## What a live institutional pilot requires

1. A signed partner agreement and named operational owner.
2. An approved workflow that avoids a separate clinical inbox whenever possible.
3. Privacy, security, consent, retention, identity-matching, and incident-response decisions.
4. A partner-controlled endpoint or existing workflow integration for structured handoffs and responses.
5. Clinical, language, accessibility, and implementation-science review.
6. A prospective evaluation plan measuring successful access, redirection, staff burden, safety, and equity.

## Proposed pilot boundary

The first institutional pilot should remain a feasibility study. It should not diagnose, independently triage, guarantee capacity, or represent a facility response until the response is received from the partner. The patient retains control over sharing, and staff confirm all patient-reported information.
